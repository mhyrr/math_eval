That is Route W's requirement — `D >= 4` (the answer can be a finite object) and `B1 >= 4` (an exact automatic checker exists) — stated in the maintainers' vocabulary instead of the rubric's, by people with no knowledge of the rubric.

Under random assignment of 3 labels among 56 problems, the chance that all of them land inside a nominated set of 4 is **0.00014**.

**This is a construct-validity check, not an outcome prediction.** Route W's conditions and the community's label mean close to the same thing, so agreement tests whether the axes were *applied* correctly across 56 blind judgements, not whether they predict anything. It is still the only place in this project where an independent party, using its own vocabulary, picked out the same problems the rubric did.
