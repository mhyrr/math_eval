## Statement

> For a simple connected graph G, the size f(G) of a largest induced forest
> satisfies f(G) ≥ ⌈√(residue(G) · b(G))⌉, where residue(G) is the
> Havel–Hakimi residue (zeros remaining after running Havel–Hakimi on the
> degree sequence) and b(G) is the size of a largest induced bipartite
> subgraph.

## The certificate

A **no** is one connected graph. Computing f(G), b(G) (both NP-hard but
exact for small orders), and residue(G) (polynomial) settles it. Exhaustive
sweep over all connected graphs up to ~10–11 vertices is standard
(`geng` + invariant code); random/targeted search reaches further.

## Why this class is in the folder

These conjectures were produced by a conjecture-generating program
(Graffiti.pc) and published as a list; the ones still marked open have the
lowest plausible prior attention of anything here — no prize, no name, no
community working them. That makes them the cheapest live test of the
neglect mechanism (`CONCLUSIONS.md`, finding 3 and the doctrine section):
if problems really do sit open for want of a month of attention, a
machine-generated list nobody polices is where a systematic sweep should
show it. 22 open conjectures sit in the same directory; this file and
[wow2-061](wow2-061.md) are exemplars, and a sweep should take the class,
not the item. **Pool update (2026-07-26, see [POOLS.md](POOLS.md) §5):**
the directory's 22 are ~11% of the real pool — DeLaViña's own archived
list counts ~213–230 open, frozen since 2020. And her ledger shows 132
historical conjectures fell to counterexamples, so the 2020 survivors are
enriched for true statements: expect the sweep to mostly *survive*
conjectures, which is the more valuable outcome anyway.
